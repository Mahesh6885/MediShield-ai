# WHO Technical Guidance: Prevention and Mitigation of Medicine Shortages in Health Systems
Source: World Health Organization (WHO) Technical Report Series & Essential Medicines Programme
Document ID: DOC-WHO-001

## Section 1: Definition and Classification of Medicine Shortages
A medicine shortage is defined by the WHO as a situation where the operational supply of an essential healthcare medication fails to meet demand at the patient or facility level. Shortages are categorized into two primary types: supply-side shortages (caused by active pharmaceutical ingredient API contamination, manufacturing delays, regulatory non-compliance, or logistical failure) and demand-side surges (precipitated by epidemic disease outbreaks, clinical practice guideline changes, or unexpected seasonal spikes).

## Section 2: Early Warning Indicators and Inventory Vulnerability
Early warning mechanisms are critical to mitigating healthcare disruptions. Primary vulnerability indicators include:
1. Supplier concentration: Relying on a single supplier or sole-source manufacturer increases disruption risk by more than 300%.
2. Extended lead time: Medicines with lead times exceeding 14 days exhibit twice the frequency of stockouts compared to short lead-time items.
3. Inventory cover: Maintaining less than 30 days of buffer inventory under volatile demand represents an immediate hazard.
4. Active ingredient single-source dependency: Disruptions at single API facilities halt global downstream formulation.

## Section 3: Mitigation Strategies and Therapeutic Alternatives
When a critical medicine faces impending shortage, health system administrators must:
1. Conduct immediate clinical risk stratification to allocate available doses to high-acuity patients.
2. Identify certified therapeutic alternatives and bioequivalent generics with approval from clinical pharmacy committees.
3. Establish communication with alternative pre-qualified pharmaceutical distributors.
4. Avoid speculative hoarding or panic-buying, which exacerbates regional supply imbalances.
