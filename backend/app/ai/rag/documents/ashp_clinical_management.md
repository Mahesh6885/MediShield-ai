# ASHP Guidelines on Managing Drug Shortages in Health-System Pharmacy
Source: American Society of Health-System Pharmacists (ASHP) Practice Standards
Document ID: DOC-ASHP-003

## Section 1: Institutional Shortage Assessment and Triage
When an acute care health system detects a supply-chain shortage notification, the pharmacy director must initiate a phased clinical assessment:
1. Operational inventory audit: Determine current physical stock across all central pharmacies, automated dispensing cabinets, satellite clinics, and operating rooms.
2. Estimated run-out date: Calculate the exact consumption velocity (daily usage rate) to predict the day of complete stockout.
3. Patient acuity mapping: Classify clinical use cases into Tier 1 (no viable substitute, life-critical), Tier 2 (substitutable with secondary agent), and Tier 3 (non-essential elective therapy).

## Section 2: Therapeutic Substitution and Dose Conservation
1. Dose rounding: Implement standardized rounded dosing to eliminate wastage of single-use vials.
2. Route conversion: Convert eligible intravenous (IV) therapies to oral (PO) administration whenever clinically indicated.
3. Restricted ordering: Place computer physician order entry (CPOE) hard stops on scarce medications, requiring infectious disease or critical care specialist pre-authorization.
4. Compounding: Leverage internal 503A or certified 503B outsourcing facilities for sterile compounding when commercial finished formulations are unavailable.

## Section 3: Vendor Allocation and Alternative Procurement
Health systems should maintain contracts with multiple primary and secondary pharmaceutical wholesalers. During vendor allocation caps, procurement teams must verify that secondary suppliers possess verified pedigree credentials to prevent the infiltration of counterfeit or adulterated medications into the clinical stream.
