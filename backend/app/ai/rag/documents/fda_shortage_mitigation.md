# FDA Guidance for Industry: Risk Management Plans to Mitigate the Potential for Drug Shortages
Source: United States Food and Drug Administration (FDA) Center for Drug Evaluation and Research (CDER)
Document ID: DOC-FDA-002

## Section 1: Regulatory Framework and Early Notification Requirements
Under section 506C of the FD&C Act, manufacturers of life-supporting, life-sustaining, or critical acute care medications are mandated to notify the agency of permanent discontinuances or interruptions in manufacturing at least six months in advance, or as soon as practicable. Early notification enables regulatory discretion, expedited review of alternate manufacturing sites, and import facilitation from qualified international jurisdictions.

## Section 2: Supply Chain Risk Management Plans (RMPs)
A comprehensive Risk Management Plan (RMP) must incorporate:
1. Redundancy mapping: Secondary and tertiary manufacturing facilities for drug substances (API) and finished drug products.
2. Safety buffer calculations: Calibrated inventory reserves based on statistical lead-time variation and supplier fulfillment reliability.
3. Supplier quality metrics: Regular audits of good manufacturing practices (cGMP) to prevent microbial or particulate contamination events.
4. Logistics resilience: Temperature-controlled transport monitoring to eliminate cold-chain excursions during transit.

## Section 3: Root Causes of Pharmaceutical Shortages
Empirical analysis of FDA drug shortages reveals that over 62% of historical shortages are precipitated by manufacturing quality issues, such as particulate matter in injectables or sterility breaches. Production delays account for 18%, while sudden demand increases constitute approximately 15% of recorded shortage events.
